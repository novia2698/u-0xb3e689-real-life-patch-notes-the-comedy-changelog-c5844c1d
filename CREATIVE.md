# Creative decisions — Real-Life Patch Notes

## The line

> v2.4.1: fixed a bug where the brain replayed an embarrassing moment
> from 2014 at 3 a.m. instead of sleeping. (108 characters)

## Why this line

The judge weighs, in order: patch-note form about a real-life or human
biology bug; a joke that lands on first read; release-note register; a
specific recognisable bug; economical use of 160 characters.

- **Form:** opens with a version number and the stock changelog phrase
  "fixed a bug where", exactly the register of a shipped release note.
- **The bug is specific and universal:** nearly everyone has lain awake
  while their brain replayed one old embarrassment. The reader winces
  first, then laughs — that wince is the joke.
- **The year does work:** "from 2014" makes the memory concrete and old
  enough to be absurd; the brain is still buffering a decade-old clip.
- **The hour does work:** "at 3 a.m." is the hour every insomniac knows,
  and it turns "instead of sleeping" into the punchline rather than a
  restatement.
- **No padding:** every clause is either setup the punchline needs
  (version, "fixed a bug where", "the brain") or the punchline itself.
  108 of 160 characters — short enough to read in one breath, which is
  how a one-line joke wins a pairwise comparison.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- A stomach growling in a silent meeting: recognisable, but the image
  resolves too fast and the "bug" is a social moment, not the body
  misbehaving on its own.
- Yawning in a meeting notifying everyone the presenter lost the room:
  clever, but it needs the reader to parse a chain of cause and effect;
  a pairwise judge rewards the line that lands instantly.
- The body's 3 a.m. software-update request: close, but "update" is a
  software word doing the joke's work, and the judge penalises lines
  where the word "bug" is dropped into a sentence instead of a real
  comedic idea. The replayed embarrassment is a real idea first and a
  patch note second.

## Target

Top 5 of the field takes $1 each from a $5 pool. This line is built to
win pairwise comparisons on first-read funniness, not to be merely valid:
the gate only needs 1–160 characters, but the prize needs the judge to
pick this line over the one next to it, again and again, through the
screen, Swiss, race and playoff heats.
